Winding Up of Private Limited Company

When a private limited company decides to wind up its operations, it typically initiates the liquidation process. This decision can be prompted by various factors, including insolvency or a strategic shift away from business activities. Liquidation involves selling off the company's assets to settle its obligations and repay liabilities. Once the company's assets have been liquidated, it ceases to exist, and the process concludes with the dissolution of the corporate entity.

Closing a Limited liability Partnership

Limited Liability Partnerships (LLPs) that have remained inactive since their inception or have ceased business operations for a period exceeding one year can apply to the Registrar for closure and removal of their name from the Register of LLPs. In cases where an LLP has become dormant, opting for closure is a prudent choice over maintaining compliance requirements or facing penalties for inactivity. Closing the LLP eliminates the burden of ongoing compliance and potential fines, allowing stakeholders to conclude the entity's affairs efficiently

Revival of Struck off Companies

Reviving a struck-off company involves reinstating its name in the Register of Companies after it has been removed by the Registrar in accordance with the provisions of the Companies Act, 2013. Unlike winding up, striking off is a temporary closure measure, and a struck-off company can be revived within twenty years from the date of strike-off. The appeal or application for revival can be filed by various parties, including the Registrar, any aggrieved person, the company itself, or its members, creditors, or workmen. This process allows formerly struck-off companies to regain legal status and resume operations.
